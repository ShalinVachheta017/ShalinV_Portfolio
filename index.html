FEATURED PROJECT / MASTER'S THESIS

Recognizing hand activities from smartwatch data

I built an offline-first pipeline that processes wrist-sensor readings, predicts 11 activities, and checks for unusual behavior in new data. The hardest part was understanding how differently the model performed on people it had not seen before.

The lesson: a strong result on familiar data is not enough to claim that a model will work for everyone.

02 / A little about me

Curiosity, then engineering.

I studied Automobile Engineering before moving to Germany for a master's in Mechatronics at the University of Siegen. I became interested in machine learning because it offered a different way to understand patterns in complex data.

After working on real projects, I became more interested in the full system around a model: preparing reliable data, evaluating it honestly, making it reproducible, and understanding what happens when something fails. I completed my master's in 2026 and continue building in machine learning, MLOps and applied AI.
