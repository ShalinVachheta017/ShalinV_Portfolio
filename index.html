01 / Selected engineering work

Selected projects

Can the watch recognise someone new?

I built a pipeline to recognise 11 activities from wrist movement and wrote 88 automated tests for it. Six people supplied the original recordings. How well would it work for a different person?

  1. People the model had seen

    93.61% correct

    The training and test sets contained different windows of movement from the same six people.

  2. One person held back

    44.42% correct

    I trained on five people and tested on the sixth, taking turns until everyone had been held back once. One person’s recordings stood out. The average shows how hard it was to transfer the model across people.

  3. What happened when I wore the watch?

    36.96% correct

    I was the seventh wearer. I recorded myself outside the original recording protocol. On that recording, the model was 85.8% confident on average and right only about 37% of the time.

What I built in response

I combined confidence with checks for unusually frequent activity changes and shifts in sensor readings. The pipeline gives a reviewer evidence to investigate before deciding whether to collect more labelled data or retrain.

02 / A little about me

Curiosity, then engineering.

I studied Automobile Engineering before moving to Germany for a master's in Mechatronics at the University of Siegen. I became interested in machine learning because it offered a different way to understand patterns in complex data.

After working on real projects, I became more interested in the full system around a model: preparing reliable data, evaluating it honestly, making it reproducible, and understanding what happens when something fails.
