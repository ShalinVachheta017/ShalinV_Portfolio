What happens when a running application loses a pod?

A small hands-on Kubernetes exercise, built with Flask, Docker, and Minikube. I tested self-healing and service load balancing instead of stopping when the app first worked.

01 / The question

Can the application keep working when one of its running containers disappears?

I packaged a Flask app in Docker and deployed it with two replicas in Minikube. A Kubernetes Service provided a stable entry point to both running copies. I then deleted a pod to observe how the Deployment responded, and sent requests to see how traffic reached the available replicas.

02 / What the evidence showed

What was difficult—and what I learned.

The cluster created a replacement pod after one was deleted. Requests were also served by different pods. It taught me that reliability comes from declaring the desired state, understanding Services and labels, and testing failure cases. Autoscaling under rising load remains a next step, not something this experiment already proves.
